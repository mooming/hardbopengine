WaitForWork

From Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp

Sleep on this stream's condition variable for at most patience. It takes the same lock the lanes are guarded by, releases it while waiting, and reacquires it on the way out.

There is no predicate, and the header says that is deliberate. "Separate from Update on purpose: a pass that found nothing must not decide how long to wait, because a driver with several duties knows its own latency budget and this stream cannot." (Engine/Core/TaskStream.h) The consequence of the missing predicate is that the wait cannot tell work from noise: it returns on any notify and on a spurious wake, and it returns after patience even when work is sitting in a lane — the caller must look again, which is exactly what RunLoop does by looping.
Five things wake it, and none of them says which. EnqueueFifo and EnqueuePriority each notify one waiter, AttachProvider notifies so a late provider gets asked, WakeUp notifies on request, and RequestClose notifies all (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp). A driver cannot use a wake as evidence of queue depth, and cannot use the elapsed time as evidence of an idle period.
The wait is on the queue lock, so a caller waiting here is out of the way of a producer taking that lock. The two configuration calls are not covered: ConfigureBudget and ConfigureRate take no lock at all, which is why they carry the thread check rather than a mutex (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp).
Nothing re-checks closeRequested here, so a close can cost up to one patience interval. RequestClose notifies all waiters, which is what bounds the delay rather than the flag being part of the wait condition; with the loop's 10 ms patience the stall is short, and with a driver that passes a long patience it is not (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp).

void. Nothing reports whether it woke early or expired, and nothing reports whether a lane has work — the caller re-reads the lanes by calling Update.

Examples

From Engine/Log/Logger.cpp, Logger::DriverLoop()

if (stream != nullptr)
{
    stream->WaitForWork(std::chrono::milliseconds(20));
}

The IO stream's driver: the logger's own thread pumps the stream it exists to feed — one Update() per turn, then a wait here — and the stream has no thread of its own (Engine/Log/Logger.cpp). This is the call site that makes the IO stream's ride-on-thread arrangement real — the stream has no thread of its own, and the thread that drives it is the one that needs the log flushed (Start).
