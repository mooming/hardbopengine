From Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp

The stream's public face on its CPU budget: "may this stream still take work?", answered by CPUBudget ::CanTakeWork — an allowance spent down by measured task time, not by a wall-clock guess.

Its wiring is narrower than its name suggests, and the header says exactly where. "Wired today in front of the general queue only, and not in front of a stream's own lanes. An entry on a lane is either a task nobody has taken or one taken and paused before it finished, and the two are indistinguishable without a field the work item does not have. Refusing the lane would strand the second kind, which is the failure this gate exists not to cause." (Engine/Core/TaskStream.h) So a spent stream keeps draining its own lanes; what it stops doing is pulling from the general queue and asking providers (R39).
Reading does not reserve anything, and the header makes the caller own the gap. "Readable from any thread. Enquiry does not act on the answer, so a reader that acts must accept that another thread may have spent the budget in between." (Engine/Core/TaskStream.h) There is no attempt at a reservation — the value is advisory by construction, and a caller that treats it as a claim will double-spend. Where the decision must be atomic with the answer, the decision is made inside the stream's own drain loop, where the same test is re-run immediately before the ask (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp).
It is the observable half of a budget that otherwise only reports after the fact. The companion reads are GetAccumulatedCPUTime (what has been spent) and RequestBudget (what the next window will allow); this is the only one phrased as a question about the future (Engine/Core/TaskStream.h). A caller with no budget wired gets a constant answer — the default stream allowance is unlimited, so this returns true forever unless something calls ConfigureBudget.
The overshoot rule lives one layer down and is stated here by reference. A task already running is charged when it finishes, so a stream can pass this test, take work, and finish over allowance: "the overshoot is bounded by the longest task, not by the allowance" (Engine/Core/TaskStream.h). No gate at this granularity can do better without pre-measuring tasks, which the engine deliberately does not do.
