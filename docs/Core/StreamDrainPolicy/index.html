StreamDrainPolicy

Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.h Decides which lane a stream drains next and keeps the books that decide it.

Class description

class StreamDrainPolicy final at Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.h: the gate a TaskStream consults before it takes work, and the bookkeeping behind that gate. A stream has two lanes — a FIFO lane and a priority lane — and this class answers one question (which lane next, or neither) while accumulating the CPU time each lane spent. It owns no thread, no queue and no timer: the stream calls it, the stream stores the result, and every field here is written by the stream's own thread.

There are two modes, and the difference is whether there is any CPU to divide. With an allowance configured, each lane gets allowance × (its weight / total weight) and the accumulations are compared against those shares. With no allowance there are no shares to compare, so the decision falls back to a weighted rotation over takes, driven by two signed credit counters. Both modes keep the same promise: a lane with work is never starved by the other lane's weight — pinned by the tests A light lane is never starved by a heavy one and Unlimited allowance serves the configured ratio (Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp).

Borrowing is free, and the header says what that costs. A lane may keep taking while the stream total is inside budget and either lane's share still has room, so an idle lane does not hold its share hostage — the test An empty lane does not hold its share hostage charges the priority lane 0.6 ms of a 1 ms allowance at a 1:1 rate and requires the still-idle FIFO lane to be refused rather than the over-share priority lane. The accepted cost is that the long-run ratio is not preserved when both lanes are permanently backlogged: whoever has work gets the CPU.
A lane overshoots its share by the length of its longest task, and "share spent" never means "nothing is in flight". A task cannot be stopped once taken, so the decision is a gate on acquiring work, not a ceiling on execution (Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.h). Charges arrive only after a task ran (TaskStream::Update() charges the lane it took the item from, in Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp), so a reading of a lane's accumulation is always the past and never the present.
Not thread-safe, by design, and the assert that protects it lives one class up. The owning stream thread makes every decision and takes every charge; the reporting accessors are const but only meaningful from that same thread. TaskStream::ConfigureRate() is the guard the type does not have itself: it Asserts that the caller is the stream's own thread, because the weights are unsynchronised fields the stream reads while choosing a lane (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp) — and that assert only exists in configurations that define __DEBUG__.
The round is what makes a spent stream spendable again, and the shipping stream ends it on a budget window, not on exhaustion. IsRoundExhausted is the question "has the total passed the allowance", but in Engine/ it has no caller outside this class's own tests: TaskStream::Update() calls EndRound when a budget-window advance was requested (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp). The accumulations therefore reopen once per window pass, which is the shape TaskSystem::RunBudgetWindowPass drives.
