From Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp, StreamDrainPolicy::ConfigureAllowance()

One line, and it is the line that chooses the mode. A strictly positive duration turns on the CPU-share mode: GetFifoShare and GetPriorityShare become non-zero, ChooseLane begins refusing when the total reaches the allowance, and IsRoundExhausted can become true. Zero — and a negative figure, which is clamped to zero rather than stored — means unlimited: shares read zero, the round never exhausts, and the decision falls through to the credit rotation.

Zero means unlimited, not "run nothing". The same convention as the stream's CPU budget and the registry's capacity ceiling: the inert value is the default, and a limit is something a caller opts into knowingly. An Assert in TaskStream::Update() leans on that — a provider is asked for work only while the budget permits taking work, and a spent stream must not manufacture more (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp).
The allowance is not charged against by this call. Existing accumulations stay exactly where they were, so raising an allowance mid-round can immediately re-open a round that had closed, and lowering it below what has already been spent makes ChooseLane answer None at once. TaskStream::Update() applies a requested allowance on the owning thread for exactly this reason: the field is that thread's to write.

Examples

From Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp, TaskStream::ConfigureBudget()

budget.Configure(allowance);

The two figures are deliberately the same number: the CPU budget decides whether this stream may take work at all, and the drain policy divides that same allowance between the two lanes. The pass that carries a requested allowance applies it the same way — TaskStream::Update() calls this on budget.ApplyRequestedAllowance()'s result rather than letting another thread store the field.
