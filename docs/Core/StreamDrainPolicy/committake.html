From Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp, StreamDrainPolicy::CommitTake()

One unit of rotation credit is spent for the lane that was actually taken from. Replenishment is joint: it fires only when both credits are at or below zero, and then each side gains its own weight. That is what keeps a light lane alive — its credit is not compared against zero alone, but against the heavy lane's, and the heavy lane must have run out too before the light one is topped up.

Credit is spent on the take, not on the charge, and the header explains why. A stream with no allowance configured never charges anything (ChargeFifo would accumulate CPU time that ChooseLane ignores), so spending credit in the charge path would starve the lighter lane for the rest of the stream's life once its opening credit ran out (Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.h).
ELane::None spends nothing, and the replenishment check runs anyway. Passing None is harmless but not free of effect: if both credits happen to be at or below zero, the call replenishes them without any lane having been served. The shipping call sites never do this — TaskStream::Update() commits only inside the Fifo and Priority arms of its switch (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp) — and the same is true of the provider path, which commits only after DrainProvidersLocked handed back an item.
Credit only matters in the unlimited mode. With an allowance configured, ChooseLane decides on CPU shares and never reads the credits, so a stream that is budgeted spends credit pointlessly. It costs nothing and harms nothing: the counters are replenished by EndRound as well.

A second consumer of the lanes — the dequeue a caller drives directly rather than the stream's own pass — commits the lane it served even though it chose that lane on emptiness alone. Every take is booked, whichever path took it.
