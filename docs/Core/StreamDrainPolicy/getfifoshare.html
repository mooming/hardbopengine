From Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp, StreamDrainPolicy::GetFifoShare()

Derived on every call and never stored: change the rate or the allowance and the next read is the new share, with no round boundary in between. The two shares add up to the allowance exactly — the test Shares add up to the allowance requires 1500 µs and 500 µs from a 3:1 rate over 2 ms (Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp) — so a share is a claim on the same CPU time the other lane is claiming, not an independent budget.

Zero means "no shares exist", not "this lane gets nothing". With no allowance configured the answer is zero for both lanes, and ChooseLane ignores shares entirely in that mode, deciding on credit instead. There is no configuration in which a share of zero starves a lane: a zero weight is rewritten to one by ConfigureRate long before it could divide out to zero.
The unit is duration<double>, and the comparison is not. ChooseLane casts this to std::chrono::nanoseconds before comparing against the accumulation, so sub-nanosecond fractions are truncated at the comparison and not in the value returned here. A caller printing this figure sees more precision than the decision used.
