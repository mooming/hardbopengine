EndRound

member of hbe::StreamDrainPolicy Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp Reopens the round: both accumulations zeroed, both credits restored to the weights.

From Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp, StreamDrainPolicy::EndRound()

Four stores, and they reset both mechanisms at once: the two CPU accumulations that the limited mode compares against shares, and both rotation credits, restored to the configured weights rather than merely cleared. After this call a budgeted stream is as fresh as it was at the start of the window, and an unbudgeted one has a full round of credit again.

Nothing is carried over, deliberately. A round is a fresh division of the allowance, not an amortisation of what was unused: a lane that ran nothing last round gets no bigger a share this one, because the share is derived from the rate and the allowance every time (GetFifoShare). What is not reset by this call is the age ceiling (SetMaxAge) or the weights (ConfigureRate) — configuration survives rounds; measurement does not.
The stream calls this when a budget window advances, not when the round runs out. TaskStream::Update() clears its window request and then resets the budget and ends the drain round (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp), so the cadence of a stream's shares is the cadence of TaskSystem::RunBudgetWindowPass. A stream whose window never advances keeps a closed round: its accumulations stay spent and ChooseLane keeps answering None.

Parameters

None.

Return value

void. No indication is given whether anything had been spent.

Examples

From Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp, TaskStream::Update()

if (windowAdvanceRequested.exchange(false, std::memory_order_relaxed))
{
    budget.Reset();
    drainPolicy.EndRound();
}

The exchange is the reason this is safe on a driven stream: one request, one reset, whichever thread asked for the window to move. The same pairing — budget reset and drain round reset — is what keeps a stream's CPU allowance and its lane shares reopening on the same beat.
