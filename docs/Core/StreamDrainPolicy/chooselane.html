ChooseLane

member of hbe::StreamDrainPolicy Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp The lane to drain next, or None - a pure call that charges nothing.

Signature

[[nodiscard]] ELane ChooseLane(bool fifoHasWork, bool priorityHasWork) const noexcept

Function description

Defined in Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp as StreamDrainPolicy::ChooseLane(). It is const and pure: it charges nothing, spends no credit, and mutates no rotation state, so calling it twice with the same two answers gives the same lane. Charging is a separate call — CommitTake when an item was actually taken from the lane, and ChargeFifo / ChargePriority once its CPU time is known.

From Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp, StreamDrainPolicy::ChooseLane()

The last row is the borrowing the class is designed around, and the source names the reason: the work exists, the stream can still pay for it, and refusing would idle the stream over a bookkeeping detail. Where both lanes are backlogged without either having share left, the preference is the configured heavier lane and then FIFO, "so the choice is never arbitrary".

The answer is a suggestion the queue does not have to accept, and one shipping caller ignores it entirely. TaskStream::Dequeue() serves the priority lane whenever it is non-empty and the FIFO lane otherwise, without consulting this function — its comment says the rate and the budget gate the stream's own loop, and gating here would let a caller's refill stall on an exhausted allowance (Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp). It does still call CommitTake for the lane it served, so the books stay honest even on that path.
None with work waiting is the only signal that a stream declined. This function reports no reason. TaskStream::Update() detects the combination itself — no lane chosen while at least one lane holds work — and counts it once per pass (laneWorkRefusals, Engine/Core/TaskStream.cpp), because the refusal is a rate and not a queue depth.
A lane with work is never starved; the ratio is not guaranteed. With an allowance and both lanes permanently backlogged, whoever has work gets the CPU, and the long-run ratio is not preserved — the cost of free borrowing, stated in the header. In the unlimited mode the ratio is honoured by credit: a 3:1 rate yields 6:2 over eight takes, and a 1:3 rate still serves the lighter lane at least once in eight (Engine/Core/StreamDrainPolicy.cpp, Unlimited allowance serves the configured ratio and A light lane is never starved by a heavy one).

Called inside the queue lock, immediately after finished items are swept and unfinished ones re-added, so the two emptiness answers describe the queues as the stream is about to take from them. What follows is a switch over the three answers, and the None case is where the provider probes and the general queue are then consulted.
