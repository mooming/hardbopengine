From Engine/Core/TaskProvider.cpp (the elided blocks are the two log calls)

Two gates, each reported, and the count moves last. Nothing here takes a lock of its own — the lock is inside TaskStream::AttachProvider, on the queue side, which is what makes adding to a lane that a worker may be draining safe at all.

The first gate is a warning, not an error, because a provider may legitimately name a stream this engine does not have. "TaskProvider name is attached to stream N, which this engine does not have. The attachment is recorded and nothing will ever ask this provider for work." (Engine/Core/TaskProvider.cpp). The unit probes are written that way, and the R37 rule is shaped around it: an attachment that names nothing registers nothing, so it cannot dangle and must not be fatal at destruction (Engine/Core/TaskProvider.h).
The second gate is the one a real deployment can hit, and the ceiling is per lane. "The lane list already holds this provider or is full, so the drain will not ask it." is logged as an error (Engine/Core/TaskProvider.cpp). The stream's list holds MaxProvidersPerLane = 8 entries per lane (Engine/Core/TaskStream.h), so a ninth provider on one lane is dropped silently apart from that line — while AttachTo has already recorded the attachment and IsAttachedTo will keep reporting true. The asymmetry is deliberate (R38 says the drain reads the stream's list, not this one) but it means the provider's own bookkeeping can overstate what is live.
It is called once per (stream, lane) that AttachTo newly claims, so a provider attached to both lanes of one stream is registered twice, counted twice, and must be unregistered twice — which is exactly what DetachFrom does, reading the recorded mask. A count of 2 for one stream is normal, and the destructor's assert is on the total reaching zero, not on it being small.
Private, with DetachFrom as its only caller besides AttachTo. A derived provider cannot register itself onto a lane behind its owner's back, and cannot inflate the count that guards its own destruction. The pair of private entry points exists so the recorded slot list and the stream's lists can only ever move together through the two public lifecycle calls (Engine/Core/TaskProvider.h).
